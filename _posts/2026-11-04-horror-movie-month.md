Here's what we watched in Horror Movie Month this year!

### October 1: [Creep Box (2023)](https://letterboxd.com/film/creep-box-2023/)

Slightly creepy dude invents a NeXT cube that can simulate the dead for about
fifteen minutes.  All anybody wants to do is turn this into a skrillion
dollars, but he's more in it for the *science*.  And also his dead wife?

It was okay, and I appreciated its general commitment to the bit, but
fundamentally: only okay.  Also, not really a horror movie, I think.  That
said, "it was on Shudder" covers a lot of choices.

### October 3: [Cat Cam (2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/cat-cam/)

Disappointing.  We had hoped for "Paranormal Activity, but mostly a cat", or
"Good Boy, but it's a cat".  Instead… it was pretty boring.  Also, it had
Jiavani (as seen on Dropout) as a totally insufferable jerk.  We didn't get
anywhere *near* enough cat.

### October 3: [Livestream from Hell (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/livestream-from-hell/)

Kevin Hart decides to become a live streamer, but is overbearing and bad at it.
Somebody at his livestream event starts killing people.  Hijinx ensue.  But the
key thing to know, here, is "from Hell" means "goes very badly" and not
"streamed from the third circle of Death".  Funny, but not funny enough.

### October 3: [Poppers (1984)](https://letterboxd.com/film/poppers/)

I think we watched this mostly because of the very provocative poster on
Shudder, and because the kid thought it would be good queer 80s horror.  It was
decent, but not amazing.  Fun to see a 1984 Spanish exploitation-ish movie,
which must've been related to the end of the Franco regime, but I don't know
the culture well enough to say.

It was very economical.  It knew that it could just not show a bunch of things,
and that the viewer would work it out.  I thought this was well done!  I also
liked the most civil "you're going to be the prey in our hunt" scene I've seen.

In brief: not great, but the best thing we'd seen so far, by far.

### October 4: [Control Freak (2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/control-freak/)

A woman has a successful career as a motivational speaker.  Unsurprisingly, her
personal life is kind of a mess.  Repressed trauma, strained personal
relationships, poorly-handled stress, lying to her spouse.  This begins to
manifest in physical ways.  Mostly ants.  And itching.  She's told to try to
relax, which stresses her out.  In the end: she should've tried to stop
scratching so much at her itches.

This was fine.  It wasn't amazing, but it delivered on what it promised.  Also,
it had a good poster, I thought.

### October 4: [Portal to Hell (2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/portal-to-hell-2025/)

I liked this one a lot!  Guy who seems likely to go to the grave with a song
unsung is at the laundromat, and one of the dryers seems to have become a
portal to hell.  A demon crawls out of it, one day, and says he's here to
collect the guy's favorite neighbor's soul.  The demon is voiced by Richard
Kind.  The neighbor, crusty but benign, is played by Keith David.  Do I have to
go on?

It got a little schmaltzy by the end, but I had a lot of fun and recommend it.

### October 5: [He's Watching You (2024)](https://letterboxd.com/film/hes-watching-you/)

Did not enjoy.  Tiresome and derivative.  "What if a slasher, but generational
trauma?"  I will quote the Letterboxd review that made me laugh:  "Show me on
the doll where he abandoned you."

Whatever, there's a masked serial killer and people think it's the protagonist,
but it isn't.  The protagonist is just weird.

### October 6: [Haunted Heist (2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/haunted-heist/)

Bad.  Dude gets out of jail and gets his three high school buddies to do one
last heist.  But he doesn't tell them it's a heist.  Also, none of them are
actually skilled at any sort of heist things?  I laughed a couple times, but it
wasn't worth the investment.  The bit about "Rat Face George" was funny,
though.

### October 7: [Dead Ant (2017)](https://letterboxd.com/film/dead-ant/)

A glam band from the 80s is still touring and is going to play at a
low-prestige music festival in the desert.  But first!  They're all going to
eat magic mushrooms.  Hijinx ensue, mostly centered around giant ants.  Tom
Arnold, Jake Busey, a little Sean Astin, and Sydney Sweeney about a week before
she got famous.  We watched it hoping it would sit alongside [Heavy
Trip](https://letterboxd.com/film/heavy-trip/)… but it didn't.

Again, I laughed a couple times, but I wouldn't recommend it.

### October 8: [Infirmary (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/infirmary/)

Body cam found footage movie.  Absolutely forgettable.  Watch Grave Encounters
instead.

### October 9: [Lady Terminator (1988)](https://letterboxd.com/film/lady-terminator/)

Was this a horror movie?  Who can say?  What *was* this?

We went and saw this 1988 Indonesian movie, inspired in part by the
centuries-old legend of [the Queen of the South
Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyai_Roro_Kidul) but also by the (then) four
year old film [The Terminator](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-terminator/).

Absolute shortest version: an evil sex spirit escapes her exile beneath the
sea, possesses a young American woman (she's an anthropolist!) and uses her to
murder a lot of people.

It was fun, not only because it was weird and funny, but because we saw it at
the [Philadelphia Film Center](https://filmadelphia.org/), which we like a
whole lot.  The audience was terrific, and everybody had a better time because
of everybody else.  I wouldn't watch it again, but I'd go to another equally
weird movie with the same audience.  Except the person who was sitting nearby
and tapping their foot the whole time.  That wasn't great.

### October 10: [Resident Evil (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/resident-evil-2026/)

I expected this to be good, and it was!  We went to the movies again (once
again the Philadelphia Film Society, but a different cinema), too.  Most of the
Resident Evil movies are, in my opinion, crap.  This was not crap.  It was a
lot of fun.  Creepy, sometimes scary, usually funny.  Good work!

At the end, a guy leaving the cinema said, "What'd you think?"  I said, "I
liked everything but the ending."  He asked, "What kind of ending would you
have liked?"  I said I didn't know, and I still don't.  But that's okay, the
movie was good.

### October 10: [V/H/S/MIXTAPE (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/v-h-s-mixtape/)

Look, the V/H/S movies are a mxied bag.  I've written about them before.  The
Halloween one is probably best.  But this was … was it the worst?  Maybe, maybe
not.  But it was a hot mess.  The early ones were built around this idea that
there is a weird cult around VHS tapes of horrible things, and the tapes
somehow become powerful.  That's just a framing device for anthology movies,
but it's sort of weirdly fun.  Last year's was just bonkers fun.

This year they *mostly* had sort of a musical motif?  But it was uneven and not
spelled out.  Between the shorts, we got GWAR.  This should have been great,
but even GWAR could not save this movie, and that's saying something.

There was a not terrible adaptation of Lovecraft's The Music of Erich Zann,
though.

### October 11: [28 Years Later: The Bone Temple (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/28-years-later-the-bone-temple/)

Quoting Letterboxd: "how many more perfect performances must Ralph Fiennes give
us before we give him an Oscar?"

It was pretty good.  Very well made.  Fiennes was *great*.  The Jimmy Savile
inspired villain should've been a stupid idea that failed, but was pretty good,
I thought.  Or, at least, he was a more interesting character than I had
expected.

I don't know that this movie is for everyone, but if you decide you won't watch
it, go look up the scene with Iron Maiden's Number of the Beast.  You won't
regret it.

### October 11: [Hokum (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/hokum/)

This was a really nicely done ghost story starring Adam Scott, who is usually
really interesting to watch.  He plays a troubled, unhappy, successful writer
who travels to the off-the-beaten-path hotel where his parents honeymooned.
It is haunted, we are told, and then we go a long time trying to figure out
whether it's really haunted.

Well shot, well written, well acted.  I liked it.

### October 12: [Afraid (2024)](https://letterboxd.com/film/afraid-2024-1/)

David Dastmalchian is a tech bro who goes by "Lightning".  He has an injection
port on his belly for "peptides".  He convinces our protagonist, John Cho, to
put a next generation Alexa in his house so it can take over his life and
control everything.  John agrees and things end badly.  Also, we're pretty sure
that there was some sort of crossover with [The
OA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_OA).

It wasn't great.  It probably could've been pretty good, but the script wasn't
there.  Don't worry, I'm pretty sure we're going to get a lot more "evil AI
controls your life" movies.  (We've had a lot already, for decades, actually…)

### October 13: [Ugly Sweater Party (2018)](https://letterboxd.com/film/ugly-sweater-party/)

This has been in our to-watch list for years.  Dude gets invited to an ugly
sweater party and goes.  One of the sweaters is maybe possessed by a serial
killer?  The budget for this movie is $192.

It wasn't great.  We laughed a lot.  It had some (bad) songs written just for
the movie, which I am always happy to see.  I wish it had been just a little
wittier, but I don't deeply regret the 81 minutes.  This is not a
recommendation.

### October 14: [Ghost Train (2024)](https://letterboxd.com/film/ghost-train-2024/)

This is a Korean horror film about a YouTuber who is researching a haunted
subway station.  ("It was built on top of a suicide cult's chapel," they say,
leading us to wonder why there was a chapel so far underground!)  Week after
week, the woman returns to ask the stationmaster about other hauntings.  He
tells her about things that have happened, and we see the story play out.  By
the end, of course, she is the next story.

This worked well.  I'm not sure it was entirely cohesive, but it had good
atmosphere and some good stories.  Also, it avoided two common problems: it
wasn't a short story forced to run feature length, and it wasn't a bunch of
shorts stuck together with only GWAR to bind them.  It was well structured.

### October 16: [The End of Oak Street (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/the-end-of-oak-street/)

We weren't sure how much of a horror movie this was.  Our finding, in the end:
not really a horror movie.  It was okay.  But it was a loser for horror movie
month.

### October 17: [Spider Island (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/spider-island/)

This was a stupid little movie with lots of CGI spiders.  But it was fun.  We
watched this in a hotel room, which makes almost any TV easier to enjoy.  The
premise is that an unethical corporation has built a luxury hotel on an island
that is a nature preserve by, like, bribery and stuff.  Doing this displaced
zillions of spiders, who will now go fatally bite the hotel's first guests.  To
make that more fun, the guests consist entirely of "influencers" who are
vlogging the whole thing.  Also, Tim McInnerny!

It was not great, but it was plenty fun.

### October 18: [Street Fighter (2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/street-fighter-2026/)

Not a horror movie, but I had been pestering Bryan for months that we'd have to
go see this as a followup to watching MK2.  It was a mess, but fun.

But also they had people in the 80s singing 4 Non Blondes' "What's Up?" which I
could not get past.

### October 19: [Tammy and the T-Rex (1994)](https://letterboxd.com/film/tammy-and-the-t-rex/)

This has been floating around my "oughta watch this sometime" for *ages*.
Finally, it showed up on Shudder.

It was … weird.  It knew how stupid it was, which mostly made it good.  Denise
Richards plays a high schooler whose boyfriend is beaten into a coma.  A mad
scientist (the guy who played Bernie, as in "Weekend At") steals his body,
scoops out his brain, and puts it into a giant animatronic T-Rex.  Why?  I
mean, wouldn't you?

It was fun, and I do not regret watching it.  Go read the Wikipedia page
sometime.

### October 20: Etheria Film Festival 2026

This featured short such as [Do Not Rewind
(2026)](https://letterboxd.com/film/do-not-rewind/) and [Wall Udder
(2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/wall-udder/).  It was okay.  Mixed bag,
like many anthologies, but not really enough horror.  This was mostly our
fault, but: look, it was on Shudder.

Maybe my favorite was
[Superconscious](https://letterboxd.com/film/superconscious/), a movie about
remote viewing experiments.  It was a bit of a mess, but I liked what they were
going for (until the end).

### October 24: [Lake Mungo (2008)](https://letterboxd.com/film/lake-mungo/)

This is a mockumentary about a family whose teenage daughter died and now seems
to be haunting them, maybe, just a little.  Evidence adds up, collapses, and
adds up again.

People talk a lot about Lake Mungo and how it's an incredible piece of work.
Gloria said she'd watched it before and was un-wowed, but that we could try
again.

It was okay.  I think it was well made.  They knew what they wanted to make,
and they made it.  I had a couple problems with it.  It had just a few too many
twists, which started to make it feel like satire.  Also, it felt like, at the
end, I was supposed to say, "Oh, *wow*!" but I didn't.  I said, "Huh."

Still, it did some things really well.

### October 24: [Ghostwatch (1992)](https://letterboxd.com/film/ghostwatch/)

Gloria said, "Now that we watched that, let's watch a *great* mockumentary."

We've seen Ghostwatch before, but why not watch again?  It's really good!  If
you haven't heard of it, check it out.  Watch it.  Later, read its Wikipedia
page.

It's presented as a live investigation into a supposed haunting of a house in
west London.  A bunch of well-known TV personalities appear as themselves.
Things get weird.  It caused controversy at the time, as sort of another War of
the Worlds.

I don't know how many times I could watch it, but twice was no problem.

### October 25: [Parasomnia (2025)](https://letterboxd.com/film/parasomnia-2025-1/)

There's a kid in an orphanage who has night terrors and has been through a
number of major traumas.  We see some of that, then cut to her present-day life
when she has things under control, and things are going well.  Until they
aren't.

Lots of dreams, confusion about what's real, and some decent twists and turns.  I liked it okay.  No regrets.

### October 25: [Uncle Sam (1996)](https://letterboxd.com/film/uncle-sam/)

I remember seeing this movie for rent back when "going to the movie rental
place" was a thing.  The poster was sort of burned into my memory.  I was
pretty sure it'd be awful, but I really did want to see it.  Good news:  I was
right, it *was* awful.

It was William Lustig (who brought us Maniac Cop) and Larry Cohen (who brought
us Maniac Cop but also The Stuff and lots of other movies I really like), and
once I saw that in the credits I was excited.  But it was bad.

A dead soldier who was, in life, a Bad Guy, is shipped home.  His body had been
lost for years.  Shortly after arriving home, the corpse gets out of the coffin
and goes on a killing spree.  Why?  He's just a *jerk*.  We laughed a lot, but
it was bad.

Instead, consider watching
[Homecoming](https://letterboxd.com/film/homecoming-2005/) from the Masters of
Horror series.  It's very different, but I thought about it a number of times
while watching Uncle Sam.

### October 26: [Maniac Cop (1988)](https://letterboxd.com/film/maniac-cop/)

How did we make it to 2026 without watching this pretty famous slashic?  Who
knows!

There's a mysterious cop in NYC who is killing innocent people.  The police
brass try to say it's a psycho with a stolen uniform, but one detective -- Tom
"Thrill Me" Atkins -- is pretty sure it's a real cop.  Which is it?

I'll let you find out for yourself, but don't miss the jawline on this guy.
The second male lead is Bruce Campbell, and his jawline absolutely pales in
comparison.  That's saying something!

It was okay.  I'll probably watch the sequel.  But it wasn't really all that
great.

### October 28: [Dolls (1987)](https://letterboxd.com/film/dolls/)

A rotten married couple, along with their cute kid, break down in the English
countryside and seek shelter in a mansion with an eccentric couple and their
many, many dolls.  Not everybody will survive the night.

Gloria and I enjoyed this one a lot more than I thought we would.  It was sort
of a little morality play, and the (notable) actors playing the older couple
were just *great*.  The whole mood of the movie was unusual, and it really
worked.

### October 29: [Dead & Buried (1981)](https://letterboxd.com/film/dead-buried/)

In a small town, visitors are being brutally killed, but nobody knows why or by
who.  Also, did one of them show up later as the new gas station attendant,
with a new name?  What even is going on?

This was one of many movies I noted while we watched the *very* long [In Search
of Darkness](https://letterboxd.com/film/in-search-of-darkness/) documentaries
on 80s and 90s horror movies.

I liked it, but it wasn't great.  It was weird, which counted for a lot, but it
needed work.  Maybe some script editing, some pacing problems.  On the other
hand, it had Jack Albertson, who you may remember ad Grandpa Joe in Willy Winka
and the Chocolate Factory, and he was a lot of fun to watch.

### October 30: [Salem's Lot (2024)](https://letterboxd.com/film/salems-lot-2024/)

Yeah, it was fine.  Totally adequate adaptation of King's vampire novel.  I
watched the whole thing without complaining.

### October 31: [Relaxer (2018)](https://letterboxd.com/film/relaxer/)

"Look, it was on Shudder."

Not a horror movie.  Dude is repeatedly bullied by his brother, who gives him
unwinnable "challenges" to prove his worth.  The dude accepts a challenge to
beat screen 257 of Pac-Man.  This is impossible, but he won't leave his sofa
until he does it.  He spends six months on his sofa.

Sometimes gross, sometimes stupid, but it committed to the bit, and I give it
credit for that.  I liked the ending, but getting there was just a bit of a
slog.

### October 31: [Silver Bullet (1985)](https://letterboxd.com/film/silver-bullet/)

We'd watched a King vampire movie, so I suggested we watch a King werewolf
movie to balance it out.  In retrospect, this was a mistake.  Silver Bullet had
its moments, but was bad.

Corey Haim plays a kid whose legs don't work.  He tools around in a sick
motorcycle wheelchair, "the Silver Bullet", made by his drunk uncle Gary Busey.
Meanwhile, lots of people in town are getting brutally murdered.  Some dude
organizes a posse to find and kill the murderer, but the posse gets murdered.
The sherrif, Terry O'Quinn, sadly shakes his head.

In the end, I'm pretty sure they kill the werewolf, but honestly, who cares?

On the other hand, Terry O'Quinn is still great.

### Other stuff

We missed a few days, mostly because I had some traveling to do.  We also
watched some relevant TV:

**American Horror Story**:  We watched a few episodes of season 13.  We may
watch more.  AHS's main problem, in my opinion, is "too many notes".  (That and
Ryan Murphy's frequent over-artiness.)  AHS 13 is too many notes squared.

**Crystal Lake**:  I didn't expect to like this much, but I did like it.  I
thought it did a really good job telling a story nobody asked for.  I would
like a second season, but *only* if it retells the same time period, presenting
an alternate take on the origin of Jason.  I don't want a continuation of the
story we got.  That was enough.

**It: Welcome to Derry**:  Out of nowhere, there was a bonus one-off episode
about the native people who lived near the woods where Derry was built.  It was
fine.

## That's it!

Some good stuff, some bad stuff.  That's how it goes!  We'll see if we have a
better mix in 2027!
